Copywriter vs AI copywriting tools

An AI writing tool costs roughly $39 to $59 a month and produces competent drafts in seconds. A copywriter costs a great deal more and produces a decision about what to say. Those are different purchases. Use the tool for volume work where a mediocre line is cheap, and pay a person for the few pages where one argument carries the sale.

This page is written by someone who uses these tools daily, so it is not an argument that they do not work. It is an argument about which jobs they are the wrong tool for, and why.

02

What AI writing tools are genuinely good at

These are real savings and nobody should be paying a person for them. If a copywriter tells you otherwise, they are protecting a rate rather than advising you.

Volume that has to exist

Three hundred product descriptions, meta descriptions across a catalogue, alt text. Each individual line carries very little weight and the work has to be done anyway.

Variations for testing

Twelve subject lines in one direction takes a tool one minute. The judgement is in which three you test, and that part is still yours.

Getting past the blank page

A bad first draft you can react to is worth more than an empty document. Reacting is easier than starting, for everyone.

Rewording a settled decision

Once you know what you want to say, asking for it shorter, plainer or in a different register is a genuinely good use of the tool.

03

Where a tool on its own costs you money

The failure is not bad writing. Modern tools write cleanly. The failure is that the writing is fluent and interchangeable, and that is much harder to notice than a typo.

A model produces the most probable next sentence given everything it has read. On a homepage that means the most probable homepage, which is the average of your entire category. Your competitors are prompting the same models with similar briefs and receiving the same average back. Everyone ends up saying the same thing slightly differently, and the reason to choose you quietly disappears.

The second cost is fabricated specifics. A tool asked for a persuasive page will produce statistics, timeframes and customer outcomes that read exactly like real ones. Publishing an invented figure is a genuine liability, and the more confident the sentence, the less likely anyone is to check it.

The third is that it cannot tell you what to leave out. Cutting is most of the work in conversion copy, and cutting requires knowing what the reader already believes.

04

The expensive part was never the typing

If you time a copywriting project honestly, drafting is a small fraction of it. The hours go into reading support tickets and reviews to find the words customers actually use, working out which objection is stopping the sale, deciding what proof exists and what has to be dropped for lack of it, and choosing the order the argument is made in.

None of that is text generation. All of it determines whether the finished page works. A tool handed the output of that thinking performs very well. A tool handed a vague instruction produces a polished version of the vagueness.

Which is the honest summary of the whole comparison. Since drafting became free, what a copywriter sells is the brief, the research and the willingness to delete.

05

What Google's position on AI content actually is

This gets misreported in both directions, so it is worth stating carefully.

Google does not penalise content for being AI generated. What its spam policies target is scaled content abuse, defined as producing many pages primarily to manipulate search rankings with little or no value added for the reader. That policy applies the same way whether the pages were written by a person, by a machine or by both. Google also confirmed in May 2026 that its spam policies apply to AI Overviews and AI Mode, so the same rules now decide what appears in AI generated answers.

The practical reading is simple. One carefully written and edited page carries no risk from having been drafted with a tool. Fifty thin pages published to cover fifty keyword variations carry a great deal, and the fact that a person clicked approve on each one does not change that.

There is a second reason to care. Language models are increasingly the thing summarising your page to a buyer, and a page with no specific facts, no named author and no verifiable numbers gives them nothing to quote.

06

A disclosure, since this page is selling something

TheStatusQ uses AI tools every day, across drafting, research and build work. A page arguing that a copywriter is worth paying for, written by a copywriter, would be worth very little if it pretended otherwise.

The line that matters is where the tool sits in the process. It drafts and it varies. It does not decide the positioning, it does not supply a statistic, and nothing it writes ships without a person having verified the claims and cut what does not earn its place. Every number on this page has a named source attached for exactly that reason.

So the question to ask any writer you are considering is not whether they use these tools. In 2026 they almost certainly do. Ask them where in their process it sits, and what they check before anything goes live.

07

A rule that decides most cases

Low stakes, high volume

Use the tool

Product descriptions, meta descriptions, alt text, internal drafts, subject line variants. Nobody should pay a person by the hour for these.

You know the subject

Tool plus your editing

Blog posts and social copy in a field you understand well. Just count the hours honestly, because they are real and they are yours.

One page carries the sale

Pay a writer

Homepage, pricing page, welcome flow, video sales letter. Where a single percentage point of conversion is worth more than the fee, the fee is not the variable that matters.

The test in one question: if this page performs badly, how much does it cost you? If the answer is very little, use the tool and move on. If the answer is a meaningful share of revenue, it deserves a person who has read your reviews and knows what your buyer is worried about.

Frequently asked questions

Can AI replace a copywriter?

It replaces the drafting, which was never the expensive part. It does not decide what your offer is, which objection to answer first, what proof you actually have, or what to cut. A tool given a weak brief produces a fluent version of the weak brief. What you pay a copywriter for now is the brief and the judgement, not the typing.

How much do AI copywriting tools cost?

Jasper's Creator plan is around $39 a month billed annually and its Pro plan around $59 a month billed annually. Copy.ai has a free tier and a Starter plan near $49 a month. General purpose assistants cost about $20 a month. Against a $1,500 monthly copy retainer the gap looks enormous, which is exactly why it is worth being precise about what the extra money buys.

Does Google penalise AI generated content?

Not for being AI generated. Google's spam policies target scaled content abuse, which is producing many pages primarily to manipulate rankings with little value to the reader, and the policy applies equally to content made by people, by machines or by both. Google also confirmed in May 2026 that its spam policies apply to AI Overviews and AI Mode. Publishing fifty thin AI pages is the risk. Publishing one carefully edited page is not.

Do professional copywriters use AI tools?

Most of us do, and anyone claiming otherwise in 2026 is either behind or not being straight with you. The honest position is that the tool speeds up drafting and variation while a person still decides the strategy, verifies every claim, and cuts what does not earn its place. Ask a writer how they use it rather than whether they use it.

When is an AI writing tool clearly the right choice?

When volume matters more than precision and the cost of a mediocre line is low. Product description first drafts, subject line variations for testing, internal documents, meta descriptions at scale, and rewording something you already decided to say. Those are real savings and there is no reason to pay a person for them.

When does using only an AI tool cost a business money?

On anything where one page carries the sale. A homepage, a pricing page, a video sales letter and a welcome flow all have a single job, and a tool with no view of your market will produce copy that is fluent, generic and interchangeable with your competitors. Fluent and forgettable is the specific failure mode, and it does not look like a mistake until the numbers come in.
