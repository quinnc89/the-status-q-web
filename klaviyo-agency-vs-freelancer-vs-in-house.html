Klaviyo agency vs freelancer vs in house

A Klaviyo setup from a freelancer or a small studio runs about $800 to $1,200, with ongoing writing at $1,000 to $2,000 a month. A full service agency usually starts near $3,000 a month. An in house email marketer averaged $102,544 a year in the United States in 2026 before benefits. For most brands under roughly $5 million in revenue, the studio option costs the least and moves the fastest.

The rest of this page is the detail behind that answer: what each option actually includes, where each one fails, and the revenue point at which the recommendation flips.

02

Why the flow build is the part worth paying for

Whoever you hire, the first job is the same, and it is the automated flows rather than the weekly campaigns.

Klaviyo published a 2026 benchmark report drawn from more than 183,000 ecommerce brands. It found that flows produced close to 41 percent of all email revenue from only 5.3 percent of sends, with revenue per recipient roughly 18 times higher than campaigns and click rates of 5.58 percent against 1.69 percent. Nearly 48 percent of flow revenue came from first time buyers, against 16 percent for campaigns.

That gap is the whole argument. A one time flow build is a fixed cost against the highest earning email a store sends, which is why it is worth buying before any retainer of any kind. The numbers are on Klaviyo's own benchmarks page.

03

What you actually get from a Klaviyo freelancer

You get one person, at the lowest price, with the least structure around them. On a good day that is exactly right. The person doing the work is the person you spoke to, there is no account manager between you, and the price reflects that.

The two failure modes are consistent. The first is that quality varies enormously and the market gives you almost no way to tell before you pay. The second is continuity. A freelancer who is busy on another project in March is simply not available in March, and there is no second person to pick it up.

There is also a scope trap. Cheap Klaviyo setups often configure the software and stop there, delivering five flows built on the platform default templates with placeholder copy still in them. That is a configured account rather than an email programme, and it earns accordingly.

04

What a small studio adds on top of that

A studio is still one or two people doing the work, which is the honest part. What changes is everything around the work.

A published rate card

Every price is on the pricing page with nothing behind a form, so the negotiation is about scope rather than about what you can be charged.

A documented process

Discovery call, written brief, build, one review round, deploy. You know what week you are in.

UTM tracking as standard

Every link tagged, so your analytics can attribute revenue to a specific email rather than to a channel bucket.

A written handover

A map of what was built and why, so the next person can pick it up and you are not locked in.

Copy written before the build

The writing is drafted first and the flow is assembled around it, rather than words being poured into a template at the end.

A name attached to it

A registered business with a public founder and a public reputation has more to lose from a bad build than an anonymous profile does.

The single point of failure is real and worth saying plainly. One person can be ill, and there is no bench. What a studio can promise is that the risk is disclosed, the account is yours, and the documentation means somebody else could take over inside a day.

05

When a full service agency is genuinely the right answer

There is a real threshold, and past it an agency wins on merit rather than on marketing.

Hire an agency when email is one of four or five channels that have to be coordinated in the same week, when you are shipping enough creative that a design queue is a real constraint, when you need paid media and lifecycle to share the same offer calendar, or when your board wants formal reporting from a firm rather than a person.

The thing to understand about the price is what the extra thousands buy. A meaningful share of an agency retainer covers account management, reporting, and the overhead of a team existing. That is not waste, it is coordination, and coordination is worth paying for once there is enough to coordinate. Below that point it is a fee for a problem you do not have yet.

06

What hiring in house really costs

The salary is the number people quote and it is not the number you pay. Glassdoor's 2026 data puts the average United States email marketing manager at $102,544, typically between $80,000 and $133,000. Payroll tax, benefits, software seats and recruiting fees commonly add 20 to 30 percent, which puts one mid level hire somewhere around $125,000 to $170,000 a year in true cost.

The harder question is not affordability, it is utilisation. A store sending three campaigns a week with five flows already live does not contain forty hours of email work. The role either broadens into general marketing, which is fine if that is what you intended, or the person is underused and leaves inside a year.

In house wins decisively on one thing that no external option can match, which is accumulated brand knowledge. Someone who has watched two years of your customer behaviour makes better calls than anyone reading a brief. That advantage is real, it just has to be worth six figures.

07

Which one should you pick?

Under $1M revenue

Buy the flow build

One fixed price setup, then decide about ongoing writing once you can see what the flows earned. Do not commit to a retainer before you have data.

$1M to $5M revenue

Studio or strong freelancer

Flows built properly, then ongoing writing from one accountable specialist. This is the band where an agency retainer usually buys structure you cannot yet use.

Past $5M revenue

In house, or an agency

In house if email is a full week of work and you want the brand knowledge. An agency if email has to move in step with paid, SMS and creative every week.

These bands are a starting point rather than a rule. The real test is simpler: count the hours of genuine email work in a normal week. If it is under ten, do not hire a full time person. If it is over thirty and spans several channels, one specialist will not be enough.

08

Five questions that separate a good hire from a bad one

These work on a freelancer, a studio and an agency equally well, because they test the same things.

01

Who writes the emails?

If the answer is not a specific named person, ask again until it is. On an agency retainer this is the question that most often turns up a junior you were never introduced to.

02

Can I see a flow you built?

Ask for the actual emails, not a revenue dashboard. A screenshot of a number proves nothing about who produced it. The writing shows you what you are buying.

03

Is UTM tracking included?

Without it you cannot tell which email earned the sale, which conveniently makes the work impossible to judge. It should be in scope by default and it costs almost nothing to add.

04

What is in the handover?

There should be a written document explaining what was built and why. If nothing is written down, the knowledge leaves when they do.

05

What happens if we stop?

You should keep the account, the flows and the copy. Ask whether anything was built inside a system you do not control, because that is how people end up unable to leave.

06

A bonus one worth asking

Ask what they would not build for you. Anyone who says yes to every flow on the list is selling scope rather than judgement.

Frequently asked questions

Is a Klaviyo agency or a freelancer cheaper?

A freelancer is cheaper on paper. A setup from a freelancer or small studio runs roughly $800 to $1,200, and ongoing writing runs $1,000 to $2,000 a month. A full service agency typically starts near $3,000 a month because you are also paying for account management, reporting and a team structure. The cheaper option is the right one until you need more people than one person can be.

How much does it cost to hire an email marketer in house?

Glassdoor put the average United States email marketing manager salary at $102,544 a year in 2026, with a typical range of about $80,000 to $133,000. Payroll tax, benefits, software and recruiting usually add 20 to 30 percent on top, so the real annual cost of one mid level hire lands somewhere around $125,000 to $170,000.

At what revenue does hiring in house make sense?

When email is generating enough revenue that a full time salary is a small share of it, and when there is enough weekly work to keep that person busy. For most consumer brands that is somewhere past $5 million in annual revenue. Below that, a full time email hire spends a large part of the week without email work to do.

What is the difference between a Klaviyo freelancer and a small studio?

Mostly accountability and scope. A freelancer is usually hired for one deliverable and disappears between projects. A small studio is a named business with a published rate card, a documented process and a written handover, and it keeps working on the account after launch. Both are one or two people doing the work, which is the part that matters.

Do Klaviyo flows or campaigns make more money?

Flows, by a wide margin per send. Klaviyo's 2026 benchmark report, drawn from more than 183,000 ecommerce brands, found flows produced close to 41 percent of email revenue from only 5.3 percent of sends, with revenue per recipient near 18 times higher than campaigns. That is why flow setup is usually the first thing worth buying.

What should I ask a Klaviyo freelancer before hiring them?

Ask who writes the emails, ask to see a flow they built rather than a dashboard screenshot, ask whether UTM tracking is included so you can attribute revenue, ask what the handover document contains, and ask what happens to the account if you stop working together. Vague answers on any of those five are the warning.
