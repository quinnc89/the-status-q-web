02

What Wix and Squarespace are genuinely good at

They solve the hardest problem a new business has, which is getting something credible online this week without hiring anyone.

Both handle hosting, security patching, SSL, mobile layouts and backups without you thinking about any of it. For a service business that needs five pages and a contact form, or for a founder validating whether there is demand at all, that is the correct purchase and paying more would be a mistake.

The cost is worth stating honestly. Squarespace Core sits around $29 a month and Wix Business around $39 a month on annual billing, which is roughly $350 to $470 a year for as long as the site exists. That is not expensive, but it never stops, and it buys a site you cannot take with you.

03

What a Shopify theme gives you, and where it stops

A premium theme at $180 to $400 is one of the best value purchases in ecommerce. It is also the thing most stores outgrow first, and usually for the same four reasons.

Every competitor bought the same one

The popular themes are popular. A customer comparing three stores in three tabs can be looking at the same layout three times, which quietly removes the reason to pick you.

App creep

Reviews, upsells, popups, a chat widget and a currency switcher each add scripts to every page. The fast theme you tested is not the site your customers load six months later.

The layout you need is not a setting

A theme exposes the choices its author anticipated. The moment your product needs a comparison table, a sizing tool or a specific proof sequence, you are editing Liquid or you are not doing it.

Updates fight your edits

Once someone has customised the theme code, taking the developer's update means reapplying those changes. Many stores stop updating, which is how a theme ages badly.

None of that argues against buying a theme. It argues for buying one deliberately, keeping the app list short, and treating the day you need a real layout change as the day the theme has done its job.

04

What custom actually means

Custom is a word that has been used to sell drag and drop editors, so it is worth being specific. A hand coded build means there is no template underneath the page. The markup exists because someone decided each part of it should.

In practice that changes three things. The page loads only the code it uses, so speed is a design decision rather than something you hope survives. The layout can follow the argument you are making instead of the order a template imposed. And the whole thing lives in a repository you control, so it can be edited by anyone competent rather than only by the person who built it.

The honest limit is that custom buys capability, not results. A hand coded page with weak copy converts worse than a good theme with strong copy. That is why the writing is treated as part of the build here rather than as something the client supplies afterwards.

05

Speed is the difference you can actually measure

Most arguments about site quality are matters of taste. Speed is not, because Google publishes the measurement in Core Web Vitals and anyone can run it on your URL in under a minute.

The mechanism is unglamorous. A builder ships its editing runtime to every visitor. A theme ships every feature its author included, whether your store uses it or not, and then each installed app adds its own scripts on top. A page built from only what it needs skips all of that, which is why the gap is usually large rather than marginal.

Run your own site through PageSpeed Insights before deciding anything on this page. If it already scores well, speed is not your reason to rebuild and you should not let anyone sell it to you as one.

06

Who owns what when you want to leave

On a builder you own your words and your images. You do not own the site. Moving to another platform means rebuilding the design from scratch, which is a real cost that never appears in the monthly price.

On Shopify your products, orders and customers stay with you and the theme is replaceable, which is a genuinely good position to be in. The lock in is to Shopify rather than to any one designer.

On a custom build the code should be delivered to a repository in your name, hosted in your own account. Ask about that before you sign anything. A build that only the builder can deploy is a subscription wearing a project's clothes.

07

Which one should you pick?

No site yet

Builder or free theme

Get something live and sell something. You cannot brief a custom build on a business you have not tested, and a designer cannot invent the answer for you.

Selling, and converting badly

One custom landing page

$1,500 buys the page that carries your main offer, built around the argument rather than the template. Keep the theme for the catalogue while you find out whether the page works.

Site is the constraint

Full custom build

Quoted to scope, when the theme is actively in the way, when speed is measurably costing you, or when the brand needs to look like nobody else in the category.

The test that settles it: write down the one thing your site needs to do that it currently cannot. If you can name it in a sentence, a custom build is a real solution to a real problem. If you cannot, the honest answer is that a theme and better copy will get you further for less money.

Frequently asked questions

Is a custom website worth it compared to a Shopify theme?

It is worth it when the site has to do something a theme was not built to do. A premium Shopify theme costs $180 to $400 once and covers a standard catalogue store well. A custom build starts at $1,500 for a single landing page, and larger multi page sites are quoted to scope because no two of them are the same shape. Pay the difference when you need a specific conversion path, a custom product configurator, unusual content, or page speed a heavy theme cannot reach.

How much does a Shopify theme cost?

Premium themes on the Shopify Theme Store generally run $180 to $400 as a one time purchase, with a few popular ones priced slightly above that. Shopify also publishes free first party themes. The theme price is separate from the Shopify subscription itself and from any apps the theme expects you to install.

Is Wix or Squarespace cheaper than a custom site?

Much cheaper up front and not always cheaper over time. Squarespace's Core plan is about $29 a month and Wix's Business plan about $39 a month on annual billing, which is roughly $350 to $470 a year, forever. A custom landing page is $1,500 once and then costs only hosting. Over five years the totals converge, though a builder still wins on cash flow in year one.

When should I not pay for a custom website?

When you do not yet know what the site needs to say. A custom build turns decisions into code, so it is the wrong purchase while positioning, offers and pricing are still moving. Launch on a theme or a builder, sell something, learn what converts, then build the custom version around what you learned.

Do I own a custom website if I stop working with the agency?

You should own it completely. Ask before signing whether the code is delivered to a repository you control, whether hosting is in your account, and whether anything depends on a licence held by the builder. With a theme or a builder you own your content but you are renting the platform, and moving off it means rebuilding.

Does page speed actually affect sales?

It affects both ranking and conversion, and it is one of the few site qualities Google measures publicly through Core Web Vitals. The practical difference between a theme and a hand coded page is usually how much unused JavaScript and how many third party apps load on every view. A theme carrying a page builder plus six apps is doing far more work per visit than a page built only from what it needs.
