AI receptionist vs answering service vs voicemail

Voicemail costs nothing and takes a message the caller may never leave. A live answering service costs roughly $200 to $600 a month at small business volumes and also takes a message, though a person takes it. An AI receptionist is $1,500 to set up and $800 a month, and instead of taking a message it asks your qualifying questions and books the appointment. The right choice depends almost entirely on your call volume.

Below is the cost comparison, what each one does with a caller in practice, and the volume at which each becomes the correct purchase.

02

What a missed call actually costs, with the numbers that hold up

This subject attracts some of the least reliable statistics on the internet, so here is only what has a traceable source behind it.

CallRail reports that on average 28 percent of all calls to businesses go unanswered, drawn from its own customer call data. The same piece cites LeanData's finding that 78 percent of customers buy from the first company that responds to their enquiry, and Small Biz Trends' finding that 60 percent of small business customers prefer to phone when they are ready to book or buy.

Put those together and the shape of the problem is clear enough without exaggerating it. A meaningful share of your calls are already going unanswered, the people making them chose the phone deliberately, and being first to respond is most of the contest.

You will also see confident claims that 86 percent or 90 percent of callers hang up rather than leave a voicemail. Those figures circulate widely and none of the versions we could trace name a study, a sample or a year. They are quoted here only to say that they should not be relied on, including when somebody selling you call software quotes them.

03

When voicemail is genuinely fine

Voicemail is free, and free is a strong argument at low volume. If you take four or five calls a week and you return them within the hour, you do not have a problem worth $800 a month to solve.

Its weakness is specific rather than general. Voicemail asks the caller to perform a task, state a problem to a machine, and then wait an unknown length of time, at the exact moment they had decided to act. Callers who have three other numbers to try tend to try them.

Before spending anything, do the cheap version of the diagnosis. Look at your call log for the last month, count the calls that went unanswered, and count how many of those people you eventually spoke to. If that gap is small, keep your money.

04

What a live answering service does well, and what to check

A person on the phone is still the best available option for a caller who is upset, confused or describing something unusual. That is worth real money, and no software matches it. The things to check are all in the billing.

The rounding increment

A forty second call billed in one minute increments is a sixty second call. On a per minute plan the rounding rule is often worth more than the rate.

Overage rates

The advertised price usually covers a bundle of minutes. Ask what a minute costs once the bundle is gone, because that is the price you pay in your busiest month.

What counts as a billable minute

Some providers bill hold time, wrap up time and the call to relay the message to you. Ask for it in writing.

Whether they can book

Many services take messages and do not touch your calendar. If the outcome is still a callback, you have paid for a politer voicemail.

The structural issue is that cost scales with success. A good month means more calls, more minutes and a larger invoice, which is an awkward incentive to carry.

05

What an AI receptionist actually does on a call

It answers immediately, greets the caller in your business's name, asks the questions you decided matter, checks your live calendar, offers real times, and writes the booking in. The caller hangs up with an appointment rather than a promise.

The qualifying step is the part that earns the fee. A receptionist that asks what the job is, where it is, and roughly when it is needed can sort a serious enquiry from a price shopper before the appointment exists, which is work you would otherwise do yourself at the end of the day.

The flat price is the other structural difference. At $800 a month the cost does not move when call volume doubles, so a busy month is simply a better month rather than a larger invoice.

06

When an AI receptionist is the wrong purchase

There are four situations where it should not be sold to you, and they are worth naming because most pages on this subject do not.

The first is low volume. Under roughly twenty calls a month the arithmetic does not work and a returned call costs you nothing. The second is genuinely complex first contact, where the caller is describing a problem that has no standard shape and the whole value of the call is a person understanding it. The third is anything clinical, legal or safety related, where a confidently wrong answer is a serious matter rather than an inconvenience. The fourth is a business whose customers phone specifically because they want to speak to the owner.

Where it does fit is repeatable booking work: trades, clinics, salons, studios, home services and any practice where most calls are a person trying to get on the schedule.

07

Pick by call volume, not by preference

Under 20 calls a month

Voicemail and a habit

Set a rule that voicemails are returned within the hour during working days. That single habit beats most paid options at this volume and costs nothing.

20 to 200 calls a month

AI receptionist

This is the band where a flat $800 a month is cheaper than per minute billing and where enough of the calls are the same shape for a script to handle them properly.

Complex or sensitive calls

Live answering service

If a wrong answer matters, or the caller is usually distressed, pay for a person. The higher per minute cost is buying judgement, and judgement is what those calls need.

The two can also be combined. The assistant handles booking calls and anything outside its script transfers to a person or to you, which keeps the cheap path cheap without leaving the hard calls unattended.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between an AI receptionist and an answering service?

An answering service is staffed by people who take a message and pass it on. An AI receptionist is software that answers the call, asks your qualifying questions, and writes the appointment straight into your calendar. The practical difference is what the caller has at the end of the call. With a message service they are still waiting for you. With a booking assistant they have a time.

How much does an AI receptionist cost?

TheStatusQ charges $1,500 to set one up and $800 per month to run it. The setup covers the call script, the qualifying questions, the calendar connection and the testing. Larger builds that add automated follow up, CRM writeback and SMS, or a complete funnel around the phone line, are quoted per build.

How much does a live answering service cost?

Published vendor rates in 2026 commonly sit between $0.65 and $1.75 per minute, or roughly $200 to $600 a month for a traditional live service at small business volumes. Watch the billing detail rather than the headline rate, because setup fees, per minute rounding, overage rates and holiday surcharges are where the real monthly figure is decided.

Is voicemail good enough for a small business?

Only if your callers are patient and have no alternative. Voicemail asks the caller to do work and then wait, at exactly the moment they were ready to buy. CallRail reports that on average 28 percent of calls to businesses go unanswered, and cites LeanData's finding that 78 percent of customers buy from the first company that responds. Voicemail guarantees you are not first.

Will an AI receptionist annoy my callers?

It can, if it is built to deflect rather than to help. The two things that decide it are whether the assistant can actually complete the task the caller phoned about, and whether saying anything like operator or human hands the call straight to a person. An assistant that books appointments and transfers everything else tends to be well received. One that loops a caller through a menu does not.

Which businesses should not buy an AI receptionist?

Businesses with low call volume, businesses whose calls are genuinely complex on the first contact, and anyone handling calls where a wrong answer carries clinical, legal or safety consequences. If you take four calls a week, a shared inbox and a callback habit will cost you nothing and work fine.
